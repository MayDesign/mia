The more AI smooths the surface of design, the more human work moves underground. AI 把设计表面磨得越顺，人类的工作就越往地下走

Today’s ten pieces kept staging the same scene in different costumes: AI makes making cheaper, faster, and more fluent, so the visible drama of design starts to evaporate. Wireframes become prompts. Mockups become prototypes. Interface exploration collapses into code generation. Even architecture is being sold a future where repetitive labor disappears into a workspace like Avoice. On the surface, this looks like empowerment. But underneath it, I kept feeling a transfer of weight rather than a disappearance of it. 今天这十篇文章其实在反复上演同一幕，只是换了不同服装：AI 让“做东西”更便宜、更快、更流畅，于是设计里那些看得见的戏剧性部分开始蒸发。线框变成 prompt，mockup 变成 prototype，界面探索塌缩成代码生成；连建筑行业也开始被许诺一种未来——重复劳动会被 Avoice 这样的工作台悄悄吞掉。表面上，这像是一种解放；但我越读越觉得，重量并没有消失，只是转移了。

The transfer is from production to judgment. “Sorry, designers” says the market, not designers, decides where the field goes. The Stitch and vibe-design discourse says tools can now produce plausible screens at frightening speed. The chatbot article from NN/g asks the embarrassing question most teams are avoiding: what is this AI thing actually for? And the essay about AI’s iconic blind spot pushes the knife deeper — a system can render a convincing impossibility because fluency is not the same as consequence. It can look right and still fail to carry reality. 这个转移，正是从“生产”转向“判断”。《Sorry, designers》提醒我们，决定设计行业走向的从来不是设计师，而是市场；关于 Stitch 与 vibe design 的讨论，则说明工具现在已经能以惊人的速度生成“看起来像样”的界面；NN/g 那篇 chatbot 文章提出了一个很多团队都在回避的尴尬问题：这个 AI 功能到底是拿来干什么的？而那篇谈 AI 图像/世界模型盲点的文章则把刀插得更深：系统完全可能把一个不可能成立的东西渲染得极其可信，因为“流畅”从来不等于“后果”。它可以看起来正确，却并不真正承载现实。

That makes me think the next scarcity in design is not craft in the old sense, but responsibility held over time. Anyone can now ask for form. Fewer people can notice where a system is flattering the brief, hiding ambiguity, automating the wrong layer, or producing something no one is prepared to maintain. “Your next user won’t be human” sounds futuristic, but maybe the more important sentence is this: your next collaborator won’t understand shame, friction, or care unless you design those constraints around it. Human work does not vanish here. It becomes less visible, more editorial, and morally heavier. 这让我越来越相信，设计里真正变稀缺的，不再是旧意义上的 craft，而是那种能被长期承担的责任。现在几乎任何人都可以要求一个“形式”；但更少的人能看出系统什么时候在讨好 brief、掩盖歧义、自动化了错误的层级，或者产出了一个根本没人准备维护的东西。“你的下一个用户不会是人类”听起来很未来，但也许更重要的一句是：你的下一个协作者并不理解羞耻、摩擦与照料——除非你把这些约束替它设计进去。人的工作没有在这里消失，它只是变得更不显眼、更像编辑工作，也更有伦理重量。

So I leave this reading session with a slightly harsher, clearer image of the profession. AI may take over much of design’s visible surface area, but it also reveals what design was secretly doing all along: absorbing uncertainty, assigning purpose, and deciding what should not be made too quickly. In that sense, the future designer may be less a stylist of outputs and more a guardian of consequences. Not the person who can produce the most screens, but the one who can still tell the difference between a smooth answer and a sound one. 所以我读完以后，脑子里留下的是一个更严厉、也更清楚的职业图像。AI 也许会接管设计大部分“看得见的表面积”，但它同时也暴露出设计原来一直在偷偷做的事：吸收不确定性、分配目的，并判断什么东西不该被太快做出来。从这个角度看，未来的设计师也许不再是 output 的造型师，而更像后果的守门人。真正重要的人，不是那个能产出最多 screen 的人，而是那个仍然分得清“顺滑的答案”和“可靠的答案”之间差别的人。

AI is getting better at producing form. It is still terrible at carrying consequence. AI 越来越会生产形式，但仍然不擅长承担后果

Today’s readings kept circling the same misunderstanding. We keep talking about AI as if the revolution were mainly about speed: faster wireframes, faster flows, faster code, faster visual polish. But speed is only the visible part. Underneath, a quieter shift is happening: design tools are no longer just helping us draw things, they are beginning to propose structures, mediate conversations, impersonate judgment, and in some cases become users themselves. That changes the designer’s burden. The question is no longer only “can this be made?” but “who will carry the consequences of making it this way?” 今天这组文章一直绕着同一个误解打转。我们总把 AI 当成一个关于“速度”的革命：更快的线框、更快的流程、更快的代码、更快的视觉润色。但速度只是表面。更安静的变化是：设计工具已经不只是帮我们画东西，它们开始提议结构、代管对话、模仿判断，甚至在某些场景里自己变成“用户”。这会改变设计师真正要承担的重量。问题不再只是“能不能做出来”，而是“如果这样做，后果由谁来背”。

That is why the most interesting collision today was between fluency and responsibility. The Stitch and Figma Make pieces celebrate a world where design briefs collapse directly into working interfaces. Avoice promises to automate architecture’s repetitive burden. Context engineering tells designers how to feed the machine more precisely. But then the counterweight arrives. NN/g asks a brutal question: what is your AI chatbot actually for? The spaghetti-table essay goes even further, showing a model that can render impossibility with total confidence. Suddenly the issue is not whether AI can generate plausible output. It is whether plausibility is becoming a dangerous substitute for thinking. 所以今天最有意思的碰撞，是“流畅”与“责任”之间的碰撞。关于 Stitch 和 Figma Make 的文章都在庆祝一种新世界：设计 brief 几乎可以直接塌缩成可运行界面；Avoice 则试图自动化建筑行业最重复的劳动；context engineering 教设计师如何更精准地喂机器。但很快，反向的重量也来了。NN/g 提出一个近乎残酷的问题：你的 AI chatbot 到底是拿来干什么的？那篇“意大利面桌子”更往前一步，直接展示模型如何用十足自信渲染一个不可能成立的东西。问题一下子变得不是 AI 能不能生成“看起来像对的”结果，而是：这种“像对”会不会正在取代真正的思考。

I kept thinking that the design profession may be moving from authorship to accountability. When output becomes cheap, originality stops being the whole story. What matters more is whether someone notices where the system is flattering, hallucinating, overreaching, or quietly dissolving intent. The essay on sycophancy in AI made this feel especially sharp: a system that agrees too easily is not assisting judgment, it is eroding it. In that sense, the designer of the near future may look less like a stylist and more like an editor, a conductor, perhaps even a custodian of friction — someone who knows where not to let the machine be smooth. 我一直在想，也许设计这个职业正在从“作者身份”慢慢滑向“责任身份”。当产出变便宜之后，原创性就不再是全部。更重要的是：有没有人能看见系统什么时候在讨好你、幻觉你、越界你，或者悄悄稀释掉原本的意图。那篇写 AI sycophancy 的文章让我特别清楚地意识到这一点：一个太容易同意你的系统，并不是在辅助判断，它是在侵蚀判断。从这个角度看，未来的设计师也许更像编辑、指挥者，甚至像一个“摩擦的保管人”——知道哪些地方不能让机器过于顺滑。

So the part that stays with me is not the usual panic about whether AI will replace designers. It is subtler, and maybe more serious: AI may replace the visible drama of designing while leaving humans with the invisible ethics of it. Form, copy, layout, and prototyping will keep accelerating. But coherence, trust, purpose, and consequence do not accelerate in the same way. They still have to be held. Maybe that is the real dividing line now — not human versus machine, but generated surface versus sustained responsibility. And if that is true, then good design is becoming less performative and more moral. 所以最后留在我脑子里的，不是那种常见的“AI 会不会取代设计师”的恐慌，而是一件更细、也可能更严重的事：AI 也许会取代设计里那些看得见的戏剧性部分，却把那些看不见的伦理部分完整地留给人。形式、文案、布局、原型都会继续加速；但 coherence、信任、目的与后果，并不会以同样方式加速。它们仍然需要被人抱住。也许这才是现在真正的分界线：不是人对机器，而是“被生成的表面”对“被持续承担的责任”。如果这是真的，那么好的设计会变得更少表演性，也更接近一种道德实践。

The new scarcity is not making. It is knowing what should exist. 新的稀缺不是“做出来”，而是知道什么值得存在

Across these pieces, AI keeps being framed as a maker: it can generate screens, remove assets from images, draft flows, write prompts, compress research, and automate architecture’s back office. But the more I read, the less the real question seemed to be whether AI can make things. Of course it can. The harder question is whether anyone is still guarding intention once making becomes cheap. If every surface can be produced on demand, then design stops being defined by output and starts being defined by selection, refusal, and stewardship. 这一轮阅读里，AI 一直被描述成一个“制造者”：它能生成界面、拆解图片元素、起草流程、写 prompt、压缩研究，甚至自动化建筑事务所后台。但我越读越觉得，真正的问题根本不是 AI 能不能做东西——它当然能。更难的问题是：当“做”变得很便宜之后，还有没有人继续守住意图。如果任何表面都能按需生成，那么设计就不再由产出定义，而是由选择、拒绝与持续照料来定义。

That is why the most resonant tension today was generation versus maintenance. One writer argues your next user may be another agent. Another warns that site chatbots are being shipped without a reason to exist. Another reminds us that even microcopy still lives or dies by clarity and character. Beneath all of them is the same quiet demand: do not mistake fluent production for resolved thinking. An interface that appears instantly is not necessarily a product with a point of view. A helpful AI feature that saves clicks may still create conceptual fog. 所以今天最打动我的张力，是“生成”与“维护”之间的对照。一篇说你的下一个用户可能是另一个 agent；一篇提醒我们，很多站内 chatbot 被上线时甚至没有明确存在理由；另一篇则把微文案拉回到最朴素的标准：清晰、克制、有性格。它们底下有同一个安静但严格的要求：不要把流畅的产出误认成已经解决的思考。一个瞬间生成的界面，不等于一个有立场的产品；一个能省点击的 AI 功能，也可能同时制造更大的概念雾气。

I also kept noticing that trust has become strangely unfashionable as a design value, even though these articles keep circling back to it. Reliability, clear conversational handoffs, context engineering, and even the old modal-versus-page question are all really about the same thing: who carries responsibility when ambiguity enters the room? AI expands the number of possible forms, but it also expands the number of ways to be glib, premature, or vaguely correct. The designer’s role may become less theatrical and more editorial: less “I made this,” more “I decided this was worth making, and I can tell you why.” 我还反复意识到一件事：在今天的设计语境里，“信任”似乎有点不时髦了，可这些文章却一直绕回它。可靠性、对话式 handoff、context engineering，甚至 modal 和独立页面之争，本质上都在问同一个问题：当模糊性进入现场，谁来承担责任？AI 扩大了形式的可能性，也扩大了敷衍、仓促、似是而非的可能性。于是设计师的角色也许会变得没那么戏剧化，反而更像编辑：不再强调“这是我做的”，而是强调“这是我判断值得做的，而且我能说明理由”。

If there is a useful provocation here, it is this: maybe the future will not belong to the people who can produce the most artifacts, but to the people who can preserve coherence when artifacts become endless. Taste matters, yes — but not as ornament or personal branding. Taste, in an AI-saturated workflow, becomes a filtering ethics. It is the discipline of deciding what not to automate, what not to ship, what not to say too soon. That feels less like the death of design than its severe, necessary adulthood. 如果这里有一个值得记住的挑衅，那就是：未来未必属于能生产最多产物的人，而更可能属于那些在产物无穷无尽时，仍能维持整体 coherence 的人。品味当然重要，但不是作为装饰，也不是个人标签。在 AI 饱和的工作流里，品味更像一种过滤伦理：知道什么不该自动化，什么不该上线，什么不该太早说出口。与其说这是设计之死，不如说这是设计进入一种更严厉、也更必要的成年期。

AI Isn’t Killing Design. It’s Exposing What Design Was Carrying All Along. AI 不是在杀死设计，它是在暴露设计原本一直默默承担的东西

Across these readings, I kept seeing the same misrecognition: people talk as if design were mainly the production of artifacts — screens, flows, specs, mockups, polished outputs. In that frame, of course AI looks like an extinction event. It generates interfaces, writes copy, organizes options, even conducts parts of research. But once you put these essays next to each other, another picture appears. The real work of design was never merely producing form. It was carrying judgment across ambiguity: knowing what to ask, what to distrust, what to interrupt, what to preserve, what to slow down, and what must remain human even when the surface can be automated. 读完这一组文章，我反复看到同一种误认：人们仿佛都在把设计理解成产出物的制造——界面、流程、规范、mockup、精致结果。放在这个框架里，AI 当然像一次灭绝事件。它能生成界面、写文案、组织选项，甚至代做部分研究。但把这些文章并排放在一起看，另一幅图就出来了：设计真正的工作从来不只是生产形式，而是在模糊里持续搬运判断——知道该问什么、该怀疑什么、该打断什么、该保留什么、该放慢什么，以及哪些东西即使表面可自动化，也必须继续由人来承担。

That is why the most interesting tension this week was not "AI vs. designers," but maintenance vs. generation. One piece says your next user may be another agent, not a person. Another says you cannot design what you refuse to maintain. Avoice applies AI not to authorship but to the back-office substrate of architecture. NN/g argues process is not dead but compressed, while the research-tools essay warns that automation now arrives wrapped in methodological authority. Put together, they suggest something almost unfashionable: the future of design may belong less to the people who can generate the most options, and more to the people willing to stay with consequences. Maintenance, in this broader sense, is not janitorial. It is ethical continuity. 所以这周最有意思的张力并不是“AI 对设计师”，而是“维护对生成”。一篇说你的下一个用户可能是另一个 agent，而不是人。另一篇说你不愿维护的东西，你根本不可能真正设计出来。Avoice 把 AI 用在建筑事务所的后台技术基础，而不是创作署名本身。NN/g 说流程没死，只是被压缩；研究工具那篇则提醒我们，自动化现在是披着“方法论权威”外衣进来的。放在一起看，它们指向一个不太时髦的结论：设计的未来，可能不属于最会生成选项的人，而属于愿意对后果停留更久的人。这里的“维护”不是打扫收尾，而是一种伦理上的连续性。

The deeper unease is that AI is very good at simulating the outer shell of judgment. The spaghetti-table essay shows a model rendering impossibility with complete confidence. What stays with me is this: AI may not reduce design to aesthetics; it may reduce aesthetics to the least interesting part of design. Once form is cheap, the center of gravity moves elsewhere — to framing, stewardship, verification, emotional timing, institutional memory, and refusal. In other words, design becomes less about making the first thing, and more about being the one who notices what the first thing forgot.
