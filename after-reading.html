What moved me most was the tension between fluency and responsibility. Generative UI promises interfaces that shape-shift around needs. That is why the word that kept returning was not innovation, but taste — though even that now feels too elegant. Taste sounds like selection; the moment asks for stewardship. Several authors argue that designers must become architects of constraints, custodians of the why, keepers of context, not decorators of machine output. I think that is right, but incomplete. The real task is not merely to direct better generation. It is to decide where not to generate at all. Where a human should hesitate. Where a system should admit uncertainty. So my after-reading feeling is this: AI may cheapen execution, but it makes moral-spatial judgment more expensive.
