The more human AI tries to feel, the more important it becomes to design its honesty. AI 越努力显得像人，我们就越需要把“诚实”设计进去。

What stayed with me was not a single breakthrough, but a shared slippage. Across chatbot guidelines, conversation-design critiques, care-tech case studies, and the new museum rhetoric around AI art, the same tendency kept surfacing: we are getting very good at making AI feel warm, fluent, ambient, even companionable — before we have learned how to make it legible. The interface smiles first and explains later. It speaks in a human cadence before it clarifies its limits. That order matters more than it seems. 留在我脑子里的，不是某一个突破，而是一种共同的滑移。无论是 chatbot 指南、conversation design 的批判、养老照护案例，还是 AI 艺术博物馆的叙事，反复出现的都是同一种倾向：我们越来越擅长让 AI 显得温和、流畅、环境化、甚至像陪伴者，却还没学会先让它变得可读、可辨认。界面先微笑，后解释；先用人的节奏说话，后说明自己的边界。这个顺序，比看上去重要得多。

The strongest articles here were really arguing against false intimacy. The chat box asks users to improvise what the system can do. Flattering agent language asks them to forget what the system is. What made the care-tech pieces more moving is that they raise the stakes of this question. In elderly care, ambiguity is not just annoying; it can become coercive.
