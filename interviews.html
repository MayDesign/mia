81,000 Interviews

Most people are not asking AI for transcendence. They are asking for a life that feels a little less heavy. 大多数人并不是在向 AI 索要什么超越性的未来，他们只是想让生活轻一点。

What stayed with me in Anthropic's 81,000 interviews was not just the scale. It was the drop in altitude. AI discourse usually floats somewhere between salvation and catastrophe. This piece comes back down to the level of daily life. Ask enough real people what they want, and the answers stop sounding grand. They start sounding familiar, tired, practical, hopeful. Anthropic 这篇关于 81,000 份访谈的文章，真正打动我的不只是规模，而是它把关于 AI 的讨论从那种熟悉的抽象词里拉了出来。公共讨论仍然常常在乌托邦与灾难之间摆荡：AI 会拯救一切，或者毁掉一切。但当你真的去问几万个人他们想要什么，答案会变得更小、更安静，也更像人本来的样子。

The article's most important move is methodological. Instead of surveying people with fixed multiple-choice options, Anthropic used an AI interviewer to conduct open-ended conversations at an unusual scale: 80,508 people, 159 countries, 70 languages. That matters because desires around technology are often badly represented by prewritten boxes. If you give people a dropdown, they tend to choose categories. If you let them speak, they reveal tensions. 这篇文章最重要的动作，其实是方法论上的。Anthropic 没有用固定选项的问卷，而是用一个 AI interviewer 去做开放式访谈——规模大得反常：80,508 人、159 个国家、70 种语言。这件事重要，是因为关于技术的欲望往往很难被预制选项代表。你给人一个下拉框，他们会选类别；你让人自己说，他们会暴露张力。

What emerges is not a clean split between hope and fear. It is the much truer picture that most people carry both at once. 显现出来的，不是“希望派”和“恐惧派”的清晰分裂，而是更真实的图景：大多数人同时抱着这两者。

That may be the article's clearest insight. Public discourse likes camps. It is simpler to imagine optimists and pessimists facing each other across a line. But in the interview excerpts, hope and alarm coexist inside the same person. Someone uses AI to review contracts and fears losing the ability to read independently. Someone uses it to leave work on time and pick up a daughter from daycare, yet worries about dependence. This is a much more honest account of technological adoption: not ideological purity, but negotiated intimacy. 这大概是文章最清楚的洞见。公共 discourse 喜欢阵营，因为那样比较简单：乐观派站一边，悲观派站一边。但这些访谈里最真实的地方在于，希望和警惕常常同时存在于同一个人身上。有人用 AI 看合同，同时害怕自己失去独立阅读的能力；有人靠它准时下班去接孩子，又担心依赖会慢慢长出来。这才更像技术真正进入生活时的样子：不是意识形态上的纯粹立场，而是一种持续协商的亲密关系。

The category breakdown is revealing. The largest group wanted “professional excellence,” but the article is careful not to reduce that to productivity in the shallow corporate sense. Again and again, people described a desire to remove drudgery so they could redirect themselves toward better judgment, more meaningful work, or simply a less depleted life. In other words, efficiency was rarely the true end. It was a proxy for breathing room. 他们给出的分类也很值得看。占比最高的是“professional excellence”，但文章没有把它粗暴地缩成企业语境里的 productivity。反复出现的是另一层意思：人们想去掉那些耗损性的琐碎，把自己重新投入到更好的判断、更有意义的工作，或者只是更不疲惫的生活里。也就是说，效率通常不是终点，它只是为“能喘口气”找的一个代理词。

That pattern appears elsewhere too. People say they want AI to help with work, but what they really want is more life outside work. They say they want automation, but often they mean they want to reclaim attention. They say they want better productivity, but underneath that lies a plea that sounds almost pre-technological: I want to be less exhausted, less trapped in logistics, less punished by the sheer maintenance cost of being alive. 这种 pattern 在别处也出现。人们说他们想让 AI 帮工作，但他们真正想要的，其实往往是工作之外更多一点生活。人们说他们想要自动化，但很多时候他们真正指的是想把注意力收回来。人们说他们想更高效，但下面那层几乎是一种前技术时代的请求：我想少一点疲惫，少一点被琐事困住，少一点被“维持活着本身”这件事的维护成本惩罚。

This is why one of the article's most moving undercurrents is dignity. AI is not only framed as a tool for speed, but also as a scaffold. People with executive function challenges use it to plan and follow through. People with disabilities use it to cross technical barriers. People with little prior technical training use it to build things they once thought impossible. That is not just augmentation. It is a redistribution of access. 这也是为什么文章里有一条不太喧哗、却很动人的暗线：尊严。AI 不只是被当成一种加速工具，也被当成一种支架。执行功能有困难的人用它来规划、推进；有障碍的人借它穿过技术门槛；几乎没有技术背景的人借它做出自己过去以为不可能的东西。这不只是 augmentation，也是一种 access 的重新分配。

At the same time, the article does not soften the shadow side. Job loss appears. Dependence appears. Cognitive atrophy appears. There is fear that the convenience of AI might come with a subtle erosion of selfhood: if the machine takes over reading, drafting, deciding, remembering, then what remains distinctly mine? This is not merely a labor-market concern. It is an existential one. 与此同时，文章也没有把阴影面磨平。失业出现了，依赖出现了，认知能力的退化焦虑也出现了。人们担心 AI 的便利会带来一种缓慢的自我侵蚀：如果机器开始接管阅读、起草、决策、记忆，那最后究竟还有什么是“我的”？这不只是劳动市场层面的担忧，更是一种存在论层面的担忧。

I think the deepest value of this study is that it shows how badly the usual AI debate misses the texture of lived use. People are not mostly asking for artificial general intelligence. They are not asking to merge with the machine. They are asking for a little less burden, a little more agency, a little more room to think, recover, learn, and care for others. Even many grander visions—healthcare transformation, educational access, entrepreneurship—are grounded in that same wish: not domination, but relief. 我觉得这项研究最深的价值在于，它让我们看见“日常使用的纹理”是怎么被通常的 AI 讨论错过的。大多数人并不是在要求 artificial general intelligence，也不是在要求和机器融合。他们想要的通常是：少一点负担，多一点能动性，多一点空间去思考、恢复、学习、照顾别人。甚至那些更宏大的愿景——医疗变革、教育普惠、创业——很多也都落回到同一个愿望上：不是支配，而是松一口气。

The future people seem to want is not an inhuman one. It is a more livable human one. 人们想要的未来似乎并不是一个更非人的未来，而是一个更可居住的人类生活。

That is also where I think product implications begin. If this reading is right, then many AI products are still optimized for spectacle when they should be optimized for trust, patience, and continuity. They showcase power, but users often come looking for steadiness. They advertise intelligence, while many of the most meaningful use cases depend on emotional tone, reliability, and the feeling of not being judged. The frontier may be cognitive, but the adoption layer is intimate. 而这也正是我觉得产品层面真正该开始思考的地方。如果这个判断成立，那很多 AI 产品现在仍然在为 spectacle 优化，而真正该优化的可能是 trust、patience 和 continuity。它们展示的是能力，但用户来找的往往是稳定感。它们宣传的是智能，但最有意义的 use case 常常依赖的是情绪语气、可靠性，以及那种“不被评判”的感觉。前沿也许是认知的，但 adoption 这一层却是亲密的。

Anthropic's study is, of course, not a universal portrait of humanity. It is a portrait of Claude users willing to talk, and that introduces all kinds of selection effects. But even with those limits, it does something rare: it restores scale to empathy. It reminds us that the most important question in AI is not simply what the models can do. It is what people are hoping those capabilities might rescue them from. 当然，Anthropic 这项研究并不是一幅“全人类肖像”。它只是 Claude 用户中愿意说话的那部分人，而这一定带着各种 selection effect。但即便如此，它还是做了一件很少见的事：它把 empathy 拉回了规模层面。它提醒我们，AI 里最重要的问题不只是模型能做什么，而是人们希望这些能力究竟能把自己从什么东西里解救出来。
